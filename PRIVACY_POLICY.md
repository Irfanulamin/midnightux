## 3. What we collect, and why

### 3.1 Website visitors

When you load our website, our hosting provider and our own logs necessarily
process:

| Data | Source | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IP address | Automatic | Delivering the page, security, abuse prevention, rate limiting |
| Browser type, device type, operating system | Automatic | Rendering correctly, diagnosing faults |
| Pages viewed, time on page, referring URL | Automatic | Understanding what people find useful |
| Approximate location, derived from IP at city level | Automatic | Security and coarse analytics only. We do not collect GPS or precise location. |
| Campaign parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_term`, `utm_content`, `gclid`, `gbraid`, `wbraid`, `fbclid`, `msclkid`) | From the link you clicked | Knowing which campaign brought you here |

Campaign parameters are stored in your browser's `sessionStorage` under the key
`ms_campaign`. This is first-touch only, it is cleared when you close the tab,
and it is not a cookie. We also store your scroll position in `sessionStorage`
so that reloading the page does not lose your place. Neither is used to build a
profile of you.

**We do not load analytics until you consent.** See
[Section 12](#12-cookies-analytics-and-tracking).

### 3.2 People who book a call

Our booking flow has two steps. In the first, on our own site, we ask for:

| Data | Required | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your name | Yes | To address you correctly and to put a name on the calendar invitation |
| Your email address | Yes | To send the confirmation and the meeting link, and to reply to you |
| A message about your project | No | So the call is useful from the first minute |
| Which button you pressed (your "intent") | Automatic | So we know whether you want a consultation, the $399 package, the $699 package, or a custom quote |
| Your stored campaign parameters | Automatic | Attribution, so we know which campaign produced the enquiry |

In the second step, the booking calendar is provided by **Cal.com**. When you
choose a time, Cal.com processes your name, email address, chosen time slot and
timezone under its own privacy policy, as an independent controller for its own
platform operations and as our processor for the booking itself.

Once a booking is confirmed, Cal.com sends us a signed webhook. We then send two
emails over SMTP: a branded confirmation to you, and a notification to
ourselves. Those emails contain your name, email address, the chosen time, your
timezone, the meeting link and any message you wrote.

**We do not ask for, and do not want, your phone number, postal address, date of
birth, financial account details or any government identifier at this stage.**

## 4. Where we get prospect data, and what we do with it

This section is our notice under **Article 14 of the GDPR** and **Article 14 of
the UK GDPR**, which apply when personal data is obtained from somewhere other
than the person it describes. If we contacted you and you did not give us your
details, this is the section that explains it.

### 4.1 What we collect about prospects

We collect **business contact information only**:

- Business or organisation name.
- Business website address.
- A publicly listed business email address, and where published, a general
  business phone number.
- A publicly listed business address.
- A role or job title, and where it is published alongside the business
  listing, the name of a contact person.
- Publicly visible information about the business's existing website, for
  example whether it exists, whether it is mobile-friendly, and how quickly it
  loads. This is how we decide whether our service is relevant to you at all.

**We do not collect** personal email addresses, personal mobile numbers, home
addresses, or any special category data, and we do not attempt to enrich,
append, infer or purchase additional data about individuals.

### 4.2 The sources we use

| Source | What we take |
| --- | --- |
| Publicly accessible business websites, including "Contact" and "About" pages | Business contact details published by the business itself |
| Public business directories and industry listings | Business contact details |
| Public map and local business listings | Business name, category, public phone, public address, website link |
| Professional and business networking profiles, where the person has published business contact details | Business contact details and role |
| Referrals, where an existing contact passes on your details | Name, business, business contact details |

### 4.3 Why we are allowed to do this, and how to stop it

We rely on **legitimate interests** (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)) to identify
businesses that may want a website, and to make a first, relevant, business-to-
business approach. We have carried out a balancing test and recorded it. The
factors that keep the balance in favour of processing are that the data is
business data published by the business, the volume per person is minimal, we
do not profile individuals, and we make stopping trivially easy.

**Your right to object is absolute where the purpose is direct marketing.**
Under Article 21(2) there is no balancing test and no grounds are needed. If you
tell us to stop, we stop, permanently, without asking why.

To stop, do any one of these:

- Reply "stop" or "unsubscribe" to any message we send.
- Click the unsubscribe link in any email.
- Email hello@midnightux.com.
- Tell us on the phone.

When you opt out we keep the minimum record needed to honour it, which is your
email address or phone number in a suppression list. We keep that indefinitely,
because deleting it is how people get contacted a second time. This retention is
itself based on our legitimate interest in not contacting you again, and on
compliance with anti-spam law. You can ask us to delete the suppression record
instead, and we will explain the consequence before doing so.

### 4.4 How long we keep prospect data

- If you do not respond: **6 months** from first contact, then deleted.
- If you tell us you are not interested: deleted within **30 days**, except the
  suppression record.
- If you opt out: deleted within **30 days**, except the suppression record.
- If you become a client: retained under the client rules in
  [Section 9](#9-how-long-we-keep-data).

## 6. Marketing and outreach rules by region

We contact businesses. The rules differ by where the recipient is, and we apply
the stricter rule when in doubt.

> **[LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED FOR THIS ENTIRE SECTION]**
>
> This is the highest-risk area of our operations and the area where a policy
> document provides the least protection. What follows describes our intended
> practice. It must be verified against current national law by qualified
> counsel in each target market before outreach begins, and it must be matched
> by what the outreach team actually does.

### 6.1 European Union and EEA

GDPR governs whether we may hold the data. The **ePrivacy Directive
(2002/58/EC)**, as implemented separately by each member state, governs whether
we may send the message. These are different questions and passing one does not
pass the other.

- **Email to a named individual at a business** is treated as email to a natural
  person in several member states and requires **prior consent**.
- **Germany** requires prior consent for advertising email, including
  business-to-business, under UWG §7. We do not send cold email to Germany
  without consent.
- **Italy** and **Austria** apply similarly strict rules.
- **France**, the **Netherlands** and several others permit business-to-business
  email to a role or generic business address, where the offer relates to the
  recipient's professional function, with a clear opt-out in every message.
- **Generic role addresses** (`info@`, `contact@`, `hello@`) are lower risk than
  named individual addresses in most member states, and are our default.

Every message we send to the EEA carries our identity, our address, the reason
you are hearing from us, where we got your details, and a working one-click
opt-out.

### 6.2 United Kingdom

Under PECR, unsolicited marketing email to **corporate subscribers** (limited
companies, LLPs, public bodies) is permitted with clear identification and an
opt-out. Marketing email to **sole traders and unincorporated partnerships** is
treated as marketing to individuals and requires consent or the soft opt-in. We
check corporate status before emailing UK contacts. We screen UK telephone
numbers against the **Corporate Telephone Preference Service (CTPS)** and the
**Telephone Preference Service (TPS)** before calling.

### 6.3 United States

- **Email:** the CAN-SPAM Act permits unsolicited commercial email without prior
  consent, provided headers and subject lines are not deceptive, the message is
  identifiable as an advertisement, it contains our valid physical postal
  address, and opt-out requests are honoured within 10 business days. We honour
  them immediately. Several states add their own requirements.
- **Calls:** the TCPA, the **National Do Not Call Registry** and state
  equivalents apply. We do not use autodialers, artificial voices or prerecorded
  messages. We screen against the National DNC Registry and applicable state
  registries, we call within permitted hours in the recipient's local time, and
  we identify ourselves at the start of every call. Certain states, including
  **Florida** and **Oklahoma**, impose stricter consent requirements for
  telephonic sales calls, and several states restrict call recording without the
  consent of all parties.
- **Texts:** we do not send marketing SMS.

### 6.4 Canada

**CASL** requires express or implied consent before sending a commercial
electronic message, and penalties are severe. Implied consent may exist where a
business address is conspicuously published without a statement refusing
unsolicited messages, and the message is relevant to that person's role. We rely
only on that narrow route, we record the basis and the date, and we treat the
implied-consent window as expiring on the statutory timetable.

### 6.5 Australia

The **Spam Act 2003** requires consent, which may be inferred where a work
address is conspicuously published without a statement refusing unsolicited
messages and the message is relevant to the role. Every message identifies us
and carries a functional unsubscribe.

### 6.6 Everywhere else

We apply the strictest of: the recipient's local law, this policy, and the
platform terms of whatever channel we are using.

### 6.7 What we never do

- We never use false or misleading sender names, subject lines or headers.
- We never conceal that a message is a commercial approach.
- We never continue after an opt-out.
- We never contact people at addresses obtained by circumventing a technical
  restriction, a login wall, or a `robots.txt` exclusion.
- We never buy or rent marketing lists, and we never sell ours.
- We never send unsolicited messages to personal email addresses or personal
  mobile numbers.

Our third-party embeds, in particular the **Cal.com** booking calendar, may set

## 13. Security

Our measures include:

- **TLS** for all traffic to and from the site and for SMTP.
- **HMAC-SHA256 signature verification** on the Cal.com booking webhook, using a
  constant-time comparison, so that no one can forge a booking event.
- **Secrets held only in environment variables**, never in the repository, and
  server-only modules enforced at build time so credential-reading code can
  never be bundled into the browser.
- **Least privilege**: access to client systems is limited to the people working
  on the project, and is handed back or revoked at project end.
- **Encryption at rest** for stored records.
- **Multi-factor authentication** on our email, hosting, repository and
  registrar accounts.
- **Dependency and vulnerability monitoring** on the codebase.

No system is perfectly secure and we do not claim otherwise. If a personal data
breach occurs and it is likely to result in a risk to your rights and freedoms,
we will notify the relevant supervisory authority **within 72 hours** of
becoming aware of it, and we will notify you directly and without undue delay
where the risk is high. Where we act as a processor for a client, we will notify
that client without undue delay so they can meet their own deadline.

**Please do not send us passwords, card numbers, government identifiers or
health information by email.** If we need credentials from you, we will ask you
to share them through a secure channel.
